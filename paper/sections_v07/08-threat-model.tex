\section{Adversary model}
\label{sec:threat}

Robustness claims are only meaningful against a stated adversary, and the adversary appropriate to
this domain is unusual enough to define explicitly. The definition below was written before any v0.7
measurement was taken, and it constrains how \S\ref{sec:results-adversarial} may be read.

\paragraph{Evaluation unit.} What is evaluated is the joint policy $\pi = (\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3)$ of
three identical agents. The utility is the team win rate of \S\ref{sec:evaluation-measure}, and
parity is the benchmark: the rotation of \S\ref{sec:evaluation-duplicate} gives both teams every
deal from both sides, so under the symmetric seating of the evaluated variant a team of three copies
of the target has a 50\% win rate against itself, and a response's edge is measured relative to that.
The principal adversary controls all three opposing seats. A one-seat deviation column is reported
alongside it on the same deals, because a three-seat upgrade and a one-seat upgrade are different
quantities that this corpus has previously conflated (\S\ref{sec:results-partners}).

\paragraph{Adversary information access.} The adversary is white-box, with an explicit exclusion
list. It is given the target's source, its parameter vector, and unlimited offline access to it. Each
adversary seat observes its own hand and the public record, as the rules require; it is not given
the deal, the deal seed, any other seat's hand, or the engine's confidence field. The exclusions are
the point of the construction. Because the target team is three identical published deterministic
agents, its policy specification is common knowledge and it carries no private correlation signal,
which differs from the team-game models in which a sampled correlation signal is private to the team
\cite{celli-gatti,farina-collusion} (\S\ref{sec:game-why}). The adversary class built to exploit
that property is the transcript inverter of \S\ref{sec:results-whitebox}.

\paragraph{Team composition and channel restrictions.} The evaluated team is \emph{homogeneous} in
the sense that its three seats run identical policy specifications. Separately, two channel
prohibitions apply: the team receives no shared secret pre-play seed, and no seat may use an illegal
side channel, defined mechanically by the four tests of \S\ref{sec:evaluation-side} over all four
engine decision types. Everything else a seat-conditioned policy can do is permitted.

\paragraph{Adversary correlation regimes.} The three adversary seats may be fitted independently,
may be three copies of one vector, or may share a pre-play signal secret from the target team. The
first is the \emph{independent} regime, the second the \emph{synchronised} regime, and the third the
\emph{ex-ante correlated} regime; the last is the only one whose result would bear on the target
rather than on the search alone, and even there it remains a response within a class. It is the
registered principal case. The synchronised regime is retained as the continuity column, because it
is what earlier cycles of this corpus measured. \S\ref{sec:discussion-future} records which of the
three this study actually reached.

\paragraph{Scope of response-value estimates.} Every adversarial figure in this paper is the
observed \emph{adversarial response win-rate edge} of a particular fitted response against the
target, reported with the adversary class, the number of controlled seats, the correlation regime
and the search budget attached. A response's expected edge is no greater than the best-response
value over any class containing it, so such a figure is informative about best-response value only
as a lower bound, and only up to its sampling error: an observed point estimate is not itself a
certified bound. The tested responses do not provide an upper bound on best-response value, and
computing the true value is intractable even white-box. An estimated negative response edge is not
evidence that a conventional non-negative exploitability quantity is negative. The consequence is
stated once and observed throughout: a search that fails to find an exploit is evidence about the
search, not a property of the target. The v0.4 record in this corpus contains exactly that reading
error, having reported a null result as a demonstration of robustness, and \S\ref{app:corrections}
carries the correction rather than repeating it.

\paragraph{Adversary calibration.} An adversary class is admitted to an adversarial-response
statement only once it has passed a planted-edge calibration at or below the effect size being
claimed (\S\ref{sec:evaluation-floor}).

\paragraph{Interpretation limits.} The definition does not license the claim that a policy passing
every test is unexploitable, and it does not license reading the opponent panel's worst cell as an
exploitability figure. The panel measures robustness across playstyles; the adversary search measures
what a fitted responder achieves. They are different objects and this paper keeps them in different
subsections.
