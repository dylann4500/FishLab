\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Observed performance gains}
\label{sec:discussion-improves}

Against \fcheap{}, the registered baseline, \sestina{} gains \vsevenBtwoFcheap{}~pp of win-rate
edge (95\% CI $[\vsevenBtwoFcheapLo, \vsevenBtwoFcheapHi]$) over \vsevenBtwoFcheapN{} games of
material sealed before it existed, with positive estimates on both banks; the registered primary
decision rule was satisfied, and the unresolved secondary criteria are listed in
\S\ref{sec:results-seven}. It gains \vsevenBtwoIncumbent{}~pp on the deployed \vsix{} policy and
\vsevenBtwoVfive{}~pp on v0.5.

The evidence is consistent with an advantage that is not an artefact of self-play coordination,
which is the failure mode the cooperative-play literature warns about
\cite{bard-hanabi,hu-otherplay}. A single seat's upgrade is worth \vsevenOneSeatFive{}~pp against
the \vsevenSoneSelf{}~pp three-seat gain, a positive gain consistent with benefits beyond
identical-agent coordination; cross-play between three independently trained runs moves the result
by \vsevenXpGapAbs{}~pp on the point estimate; and the advantage retains a positive estimate under
\vsevenDiaRowsWord{} rule variants with a largest excursion of \vsevenDiaMaxExcursion{}~pp.

On RQ3, the largest observed leave-one-out drop belongs to one component, a half-suit contestation
weight, at \vsevenLooRtwelve{}~pp $[\vsevenLooRtwelveLo, \vsevenLooRtwelveHi]$. The stronger
statement, that this coordinate is where the gain lives, is not supported at this precision
(\S\ref{sec:results-attribution}), and the registered location criterion did not replicate across
banks (\S\ref{sec:results-location}). That component originated in a coordinate sweep during adversary generation rather than in
architecture work; its value was chosen as the argmax of a measured dose response, and the mechanism
it turned out to express is not the one it was designed for (\S\ref{sec:agent-contest}). Its
provenance matters for the next subsection.

\subsection{Null results and attribution limits}
\label{sec:discussion-notimproves}

Against \composite{}, \sestina{} gains \vsevenBtwoComposite{}~pp
$[\vsevenBtwoCompositeLo, \vsevenBtwoCompositeHi]$, an interval containing zero
(\S\ref{sec:results-composite}). The composite is not an external baseline; it was assembled during
this cycle's own development, from the \vsix{} frontier's search, the contestation weight and one
switch. The account of this cycle's progress that the evidence supports is that the gain over
\fcheap{} is replicated, that the components assembled during adversary generation account for the
part of it the evaluation can resolve, and that the evaluation does not resolve an additional
benefit from the modifications introduced afterwards.

Three further results are consistent with that account. The frozen configuration is
\vsevenRegretFrozenRank{}rd of four on empirical minimax regret over the shared panel. Its headline
worst cell does not replicate in sign. And of the four quantities the registered selection check
tabulates, the largest shortfall against an advance-stated training value falls on precisely the
group of components the configuration was selected over.

Four candidate mechanisms were tested and did not demonstrate an additional benefit
(\S\ref{sec:development-rejected}); each result constrains a previously plausible direction under
the tested conditions. The declaration-allocation channel, ranked second in the project's design
register, produced no difference in the tested comparison, because the two allocators agreed at
every recorded decision; the posited mechanism was not observed, which is an empirical finding about
these implementations rather than an identity. A leaf evaluator improved \vsevenKoneFold{}-fold in
sample in the only statistic its consumer can use did not produce a resolved change in play,
because the deviation rule that consumes it is nearly leaf-invariant, a mismatch between surrogate
metric and playing performance related to the abstraction pathology of \cite{waugh-abstraction}.
Each of the three self-oriented per-decision objectives tested produced a worse policy at matched
budget while moving its own proxy in the intended direction; the two channel-oriented fits lost
because the ask and declaration channels traded against each other in these fits. And a learned
re-ranker aimed at amortising test-time search loses outright deployed unrestricted at
\vsevenKfiveUnrestricted{}~pp; restricted to the exchangeable tied set, it did not demonstrate an
advantage over a free public-history tie-break. Inside that tied set approximately
\vsevenCurseTieShare\% of the search's own selection signal is winner's curse on its Monte Carlo
draw, against approximately \vsevenCurseShare\% for the search as a whole.

\subsection{Scope of the policy-class findings}
\label{sec:discussion-limits}

On RQ4, the tested candidate modifications did not demonstrate a replicated additional gain under
the evaluated settings. This finding does not establish local optimality or uniform performance
across the policy class. Improvements from untested modifications, different training procedures,
larger search budgets, or combinations of small effects remain possible: the evaluation resolves
effects of about a point and does not resolve effects of a quarter of a point, so a family of small
mechanisms that individually sit below that resolution and compound is consistent with everything
reported here, and neither this cycle nor the last tested that hypothesis. The precision of direct
policy comparisons, which is a property of the cell sizes, should be distinguished from the
sensitivity of the calibrated adversarial searches, which is a property of the responder classes
and budgets and did not improve with evaluation games in the one comparison made
(\S\ref{sec:evaluation-floor}).

The one mechanism that did convert was found by sweeping a coordinate, not by designing an
architecture. That is a weak signal about where remaining value lies, but it is the signal the data
provide.

\subsection{Limits of adversarial evaluation}
\label{sec:discussion-exploit}

None of the \vsevenBfourArmsWord{} adversarial searches found a positive edge against \sestina{} at the
tested budgets, in the tested classes, controlling all three opposing seats in the synchronised
correlation regime. The result is conditional on the specified response classes, budgets, and
correlation regime, and it is a set of observed response edges rather than a bound on best-response
value in either direction.

A stronger response outside the tested classes, or the same classes at a larger budget, could give a
larger figure. The white-box transcript inverter, which is the class this domain's adversary model
was written for, effectively searched \vsevenZeightLive{} live coordinates at \vsevenBfourGens{}
generations of \vsevenBfourPop{}, against a \vsix{} surrogate of the target rather than the target
itself (\S\ref{sec:results-whitebox}). Its failure is evidence about that search. This is the
reading error the v0.4 record in this corpus committed, having reported a null adversarial result
as a demonstration of robustness, and naming it here is how this paper avoids repeating it.

Converting any of this into a statement about \sestina{} rather than about the searches would
require a bound in the other direction, and \S\ref{sec:discussion-future} explains why none exists.

\subsection{External validity}
\label{sec:discussion-external}

Three limitations bound how far any of these results generalise.

The opponent panel is a modelling choice. Its deception archetypes are stylised implementations of
manoeuvres one person reported from live play, and they are not people. A gain against one of them is
evidence about robustness to a class of behaviour, not a measurement of play against a person. The
partner-substitution columns have the same limitation from the other side, since a foreign partner
here is another bot.

There is no human evaluation of any kind in this corpus. The project's standing practice is never to
present a self-play configuration as the one a person would encounter, because bot-only evaluation
does not establish performance with human players.

Every number in this paper was produced by one engine, written by this project, audited by tests this
project wrote. The strongest available substitute for external replication was used, in the form of
material sealed before the configuration existed, digests computed by a second build, and a protocol
registered in advance, and it remains a substitute. In particular, there are no independently authored
six-player Fish agents to compare against, which is why \S\ref{sec:intro-strength} confines the
paper's comparative claim to this project's own lineage.

\subsection{Implementation and evaluation limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-engineering}

\paragraph{Cross-deal state persistence.} Agents are constructed once per worker
thread and reused across deals, and some per-agent state survives the inter-deal reset, reachable only
under the search. The carrier was identified as the rollout engine, the specific field was not, and
the repair was deliberately deferred because it changes the play of every searching configuration by
about one deal in \vsevenSsixIncidence{} and applying it after freezing would defeat the purpose of
freezing. \S\ref{sec:results-controls} measures it on holdout material:
\vsevenSsixPlainNonzero{} of \vsevenSsixCells{} audited cells show a nonzero residual with reused
agents on one thread, every one of them a searching configuration, and the zero-tolerance gate
condition is clean. The residual's effect on a scored win rate is bounded only by incidental
observations (\S\ref{sec:results-controls}), not by a control cell. The frozen results are
preserved as measured; a separately identified corrected implementation with sensitivity reruns of
the key comparisons is additional research, listed in \S\ref{sec:discussion-future}.

\paragraph{Validation-mode mismatch.} The isolation tests' zero-tolerance form requires one thread
and agents rebuilt per deal, and rebuilding agents per deal changes play. The scored cells ran with
\vsevenScoredThreads{} worker threads and reused agents, a mode in which the strict isolation test
was not run on holdout material, and the residual is not removed from it.

\paragraph{Shared training ancestry.} The three independently trained runs of
\S\ref{sec:results-crossplay} all begin from or near the \vsix{} vector. A convention all three
inherited from that common ancestor would appear on the diagonal and off it alike and would be
invisible to the test. What the test rules out is a convention formed during each run's own fit.

\paragraph{Unused reserved deal banks.} The fitting driver derives its
own per-generation deal stream from its seed rather than constructing the named bank's deals, so three
banks reserved for fitting are verified and then unused as deals; a fourth is unused because the
sealed panel members were scored on the two primary banks so that minimax regret is computed over one
shared pair. The material accounting should be read as three evaluation banks played plus a derived
fitting stream.

\paragraph{Inactive action-cap intervention.} The ask-cap row is bit-identical to the default, so the
cap change produced no observed behavioural effect in this sample, and the legacy residual it feeds
is arithmetically legacy minus two effective components rather than three, with a propagated
interval \vsevenLegacyResidualRatio{} times the size of the residual itself and not a valid 95\%
interval (\S\ref{sec:results-dialects}).

\paragraph{Termination limitations.} No configuration here reaches the action cap
in any scored cell but one, in \vsevenLimitCellGames{} games, and the stall detector's separation
between ordinary play and deadlock is a measurement over \vsevenStallMirrorGames{} mirror games. What
is not proved is that the stall rule together with the stopping rule terminates on every reachable
position.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:discussion-future}

Two claims a future release might wish to make, that a Fish agent is near-optimal or that it is the
strongest known, are unsupported by anything here. Both are reachable in principle, and the evidence
each requires is specific enough to enumerate.

\paragraph{Exploitability upper bounds.} Every adversarial figure in this corpus is the observed
edge of a particular response found by a search. A near-optimality claim requires a bound in the
other direction, showing that no adversary in a stated class can exceed some $\varepsilon$. None
exists here for any class, and producing one even for a restricted class would convert every
adversarial sentence in this corpus from a statement about searches into a statement about the
agent.

\paragraph{Team local-best-response methods.} The standard
cheap exploitability probe rests on a passive continuation \cite{lisy2017lbr}. Fish has none, since a
player must ask or declare (\S\ref{sec:game-why}). Until a defensible rollout policy for that role is
named and calibrated the probe cannot be ported, and the literature's own caution that choosing a
value function is the difficult part applies directly \cite{brown2018depthlimited}.

\paragraph{Ex-ante correlated adversaries.} The adversary model names three correlation regimes and
makes the ex-ante correlated one the principal case. What has been built and measured is the
synchronised regime, plus one correlated fit reaching \vsevenCorrelatedFit{}~pp, below that class's
\vsevenCorrelatedFloor{}-point calibration threshold. Whether such a responder is buildable for a
game of this size is open; the principal regime has in practice fallen back to a weaker one.

\paragraph{Composition of small improvements.} This is the one hypothesis both this cycle and the
previous one explicitly declined to test. A one-point planning half-width on the scoreboard
requires roughly \vsevenScoreboardGames{} games per cell, and a quarter of a point sixteen times
that, while the per-decision estimator of \S\ref{sec:development-perdecision} reaches a comparable
planning half-width for a one-point-equivalent effect in \vsevenPerDecisionGamesLo{} to
\vsevenPerDecisionGamesHi{} games under the design register's conversion factors. That estimator is
supported as an estimator and unused, because fitting against it failed. Using it as an estimator
while continuing to fit against the scoreboard is the obvious untried experiment. A joint bootstrap
of between-cell contrasts, which would require re-running cells with per-deal capture
(\S\ref{sec:evaluation-intervals}), belongs with it.

\paragraph{External-agent and human evaluation.} A comparative claim beyond this lineage requires at
least one independently authored opponent and, for any claim about play against people, a
duplicate-style human battery with enough deals to resolve a point. Neither exists, and the six-seat
duplicate design for the latter has not been settled.

\paragraph{Cross-deal state reset.} The defect of \S\ref{sec:discussion-engineering} should be
repaired in a separately identified implementation and the key comparisons re-measured as
sensitivity reruns, before rather than after any subsequent configuration is frozen; the frozen
results reported here are preserved as measured.
